This is a fictional document created for training purposes.

Section 1. Purpose and scope
1.1 This policy sets the minimum service levels that every passenger should experience at the airport, from arrival at the terminal to boarding or leaving.
1.2 It applies to Terminal 1 and Terminal 2 and to the airport operator, border agencies working in the terminals, security contractors and ground handlers.
1.3 Related documents: Counter Staffing and Roster Standard (GIA-STD-03), Peak-Period Operations and Queue Escalation SOP (GIA-SOP-06), Complaint Handling Procedure (GIA-SOP-05), KPI and Data Definitions Manual (GIA-MAN-08).

Section 2. Processing time targets
2.1 Departure check-in: the average wait must not exceed 10 minutes.
2.2 Departure security screening: the average wait must not exceed 10 minutes.
2.3 Departure immigration: the average wait must not exceed 15 minutes, and no more than 5 percent of departing passengers may wait longer than 30 minutes in any peak hour.
2.4 Arrival immigration: the average wait must not exceed 15 minutes.
2.5 Baggage reclaim: the average wait for baggage must not exceed 20 minutes.

Section 3. Peak hours and immigration counters
3.1 Peak hours are 17:00 to 22:59 every day of the week, in both terminals.
3.2 During peak hours a minimum number of departure immigration counters must be open: 9 counters in Terminal 1 and 5 counters in Terminal 2. Minimums for other checkpoints are set in the Counter Staffing and Roster Standard.
3.3 If the average departure immigration wait exceeds 30 minutes for 3 consecutive hours, the airport duty manager must request additional border staff immediately and record the incident, as set out in GIA-SOP-06.
3.4 Counter rosters must be reviewed every month against the actual waiting times recorded. Any peak hour that failed the target in section 2.3 must be corrected in the next roster.
3.5 A temporary exception to section 3.2 is allowed only through the exception process in section 5 of GIA-STD-03. An exception always has an expiry date.

Section 4. Measurement
4.1 Waiting times are measured from the passenger timing sample, which covers 4 percent of passengers.
4.2 Compliance is assessed per terminal, per weekday and per hour band, aggregated over a calendar month.
4.3 Sample counts are multiplied by 25 to estimate the number of passengers affected. Averages and percentages are not scaled.
4.4 Passengers who use an automated smart gate are included in the average.

Section 5. Passenger satisfaction
5.1 The average satisfaction score must be at least 4.0 out of 5 in every survey category.
5.2 Any category whose monthly average falls below 3.5 requires a written improvement plan within 14 days.
5.3 A category is reported only when at least 30 responses exist for the period.

Section 6. Missed flights
6.1 If the share of departing passengers who miss their flight exceeds 1 percent in any peak hour band, an executive review of staffing and processes is mandatory.
6.2 Airport-caused misses, where a queue longer than 45 minutes was the cause, must be reported to the Customer Experience Committee.
6.3 Passengers affected receive rebooking assistance under section 4 of GIA-SOP-05.

Section 7. Governance
7.1 The Passenger Experience team publishes a monthly compliance report to the Customer Experience Committee by the 10th of the following month.
7.2 This policy is reviewed once a year.
